Explanation Text Learning Material

Understanding natural phenomena through English texts

What is an Explanation Text?

An Explanation Text is a nonfiction text that tells how or why something happens. It focuses on physical, natural, social, or cultural phenomena (such as forest fires, rain cycles, earthquakes, or volcanic eruptions).

Generic Structure

  • 1. General Statement: Stating the phenomenon being explained or giving a general outline.
  • 2. Sequenced Explanation: A series of paragraphs explaining how or why the events occur step-by-step.
  • 3. Closing (Optional): Concluding remarks or summary.

Example Text: How Wildfires Occur

Reading Sample

General Statement: A wildfire is an unplanned, uncontrolled fire that burns in natural areas such as forests, brush, or grasslands. Wildfires can cause severe damage to the environment, wildlife habitats, and human settlements.

Sequenced Explanation 1 (Causes): Wildfires usually occur during dry seasons when temperatures are extremely high. They can be triggered by natural factors like lightning strikes hitting dry trees, or human activities such as unextinguished campfires, discarded cigarette butts, or land-clearing burning practices.

Sequenced Explanation 2 (Process): Once a spark ignites dry leaves, twigs, or peatland, strong winds fan the flames, causing the fire to spread rapidly across the forest. As combustible materials burn, enormous amounts of thick smoke and greenhouse gases are released into the atmosphere.

Conclusion: Therefore, preventing wildfires through strict regulations and careful community awareness is crucial for protecting our earth and ecological balance.
